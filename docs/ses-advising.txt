Boston MS-SES advising guidance

Program calendar and course load

- The College of Engineering (COE) uses semesters. Fall and spring are the
  main teaching terms. Summer is a single semester from May through August
  starting in 2026, with courses that can run for only part of the term.
  MS-SES typically admits in fall and spring. The 32-credit degree can be
  completed in four full-time academic semesters. Enrollment dates, course
  availability, a part-time pace, and co-op can affect the calendar timeline.
- Use a 9-credit ceiling for standard MS-SES term plans. The normal full-time
  academic load is 8 credits, typically two 4-credit courses. Students planning
  co-op may add the 1-credit ENCP 6000 course for a 9-credit schedule when an
  eligible section fits. The Boston welcome guide limits the regular academic
  load to two courses and rules out academic overload exceptions in the first
  year. The SEIS FAQ describes possible final-term overloads for eligible
  students. Use an overload exception only when approval is confirmed.
- The program offers part-time study. U.S. citizens and permanent residents
  can consider part-time enrollment if it fits their program status. COE has
  no general minimum credit load for officially part-time students. One
  4-credit course is a possible starting point. Confirm individual enrollment
  constraints with academic advising when relevant.

F-1 and J-1 enrollment for the Boston program

- In a required academic term, the usual full-time load is 8 credits with
  qualifying on-ground enrollment throughout the term. Prefer sections in
  Boston that meet the on-ground requirement. Check instructional method and
  dates since a single half-term course cannot cover the whole semester. OGS
  identifies Traditional, Hybrid, and Live Cast as qualifying F-1 instructional
  methods. Verify unclear designations with advising.
- For a standard F-1 plan of two 4-credit courses, at least one course must
  provide qualifying on-ground enrollment throughout the term, or on-ground
  sections must collectively cover it. Up to one eligible 4-credit online
  course can count toward that 8-credit load. J-1 students must meet the
  full-time minimum through on-ground coursework before adding online courses.
  Direct either group to academic advising before enrolling online.
- A continuing student's summer may be optional. Check whether summer is the
  first or final term, whether a new I-20 or DS-2019 starts in summer, or whether
  the program requires summer enrollment. Refer reduced course loads to OGS
  for advance authorization. In the final term, a single remaining course must
  be in person. Refer qualifying stipended assistantships (normally a 6-credit
  minimum), co-op/CPT, and other special circumstances to academic advising
  and OGS for the applicable rules. A schedule check cannot verify authorization.

Co-op preparation

- Students pursuing co-op must complete ENCP 6000 (Career Management for
  Engineers, 1 credit) to qualify for a placement, unless the program has
  approved a waiver. The course is optional for students who do not pursue
  co-op and is separate from the 32 credits required for the degree.
- Look for an ENCP 6000 section designated for Boston MS-SES in Banner during
  the first or second semester. Starting a co-op placement and taking the
  preparation course are separate milestones. The Boston welcome guide says
  students cannot start a co-op during their first two semesters. Refer other
  eligibility and search-timing questions to the program's co-op coordinator.

First-semester plan

This section applies to students entering the Boston program in Spring 2027.
Source: Spring 2027 Course Registration Guidance (docs/ses-welcome.txt).

- Start with CSYE 6200 (Concepts of Object-Oriented Design), the required
  first-semester course for this cohort.
- Pair CSYE 6200 with one recommended elective that fits the student's goals
  and experience. Schedule CSYE 7230 (Software Engineering) and INFO 6205
  (Program Structure and Algorithms) from the second semester onward, subject
  to prerequisites and course availability.
- Use Boston sections for first-semester recommendations. If a schedule works
  only with an Online section, label it as provisional and have the student
  confirm with program advising that the section is allowed for this cohort.
- If CSYE 6200 cannot fit, explain the blocker and suggest checking with program
  advising before proceeding with an elective-only schedule.

Recommended electives

- CSYE 7280: User experience design and testing, including usability and QA.
- DAMG 6210: Database design and data management. Suitable for students new to
  databases and interested in data management or business intelligence.
- INFO 6105: Data science and machine learning. Best suited to students with
  prior programming or development experience. Check the prerequisite and
  concurrent enrollment rules before pairing it with CSYE 6200.
- INFO 6150: Front-end web development and user interfaces. Suitable for
  students with limited programming experience who want to learn JavaScript
  and HTML.

AI learning roadmap for continuing students

- INFO 6105 covers model training and evaluation and is a useful ML foundation.
  For a student who needs more Python practice, DAMG 6105 is a foundation
  option. Use INFO 6205 for algorithmic depth and plan CSYE 7230 around
  availability so the remaining core requirements stay on track.
- CSYE 7380 fits an interest in generative models, including transformers and
  practical model-building. Check its own prerequisites. Prior ML study can be
  useful preparation even when the catalog offers another eligibility route.
- CSYE 7105 fits an interest in parallel ML and performance. DAMG 7245 fits an
  interest in large-scale data pipelines and deployment. INFO 7390 focuses on
  data integration, transformation, and architecture. Recheck each course's
  prerequisites and preserve alternative qualifying paths. For example, the
  2026–2027 catalog allows DAMG 6105 or INFO 6105 with a B for CSYE 7105 and
  DAMG 7245, while INFO 7390 requires INFO 6105 with a B.

Use the published catalog tools for prerequisites, campus, class times, CRNs,
and seats. The welcome guide's section listings describe a point in time.
